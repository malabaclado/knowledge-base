> [!definition] Factor
> Let $f(x), g(x) \in F[x]$. We say $g(x)$ is a **factor** of $f(x)$ if $$f(x)=g(x)h(x)$$ for some $h(x)\in F[x]$.


In this case, we also say that *g(x) divides f(x)* written as $g(x)|f(x)$.
	

> [!definition] Factor Theorem
> Let $F$ be a field, $a\in F$ and $f(x)\in F[x]$. Then **a is a zero** of $f(x)$ if and only if $x-a$ is a factor of $f(x)$.


> [!theorem] Corollary 1
> Let F be a field. If  $f(x) \in F[x]$ is nonconstant, then f(x) has at most n zeros.

> [!theorem] Corollary 2
> If G is a finite multiplicative subgoup of F* over polynomial multiplication, then G is cyclic. In particaular, the multiplicative group of all nonzero elements of a finite field is cyclic.
