The Dirac delta function, also known as the [[unit impulse]] is a generalized function or distribution over the real numbers whose value is zero everywhere except at zero, and whose integral over the entire real line is equal to one.

![[Dirac delta function.png|300]]

The graph of the Dirac delta is usually thought of as following the whole x-axis and the positive y-axis.

Dirac delta function is represented by $\delta(x)$.

# Properties
The Dirac delta function has a wide range of properties that can help you in evaluating integrals, simplifying differential equations, and applying them to model impulse functions, along with other applications.

The three main properties that you need to be aware of are shown below.

> [!NOTE] **Property 1**
>  The dirac delta function $\delta(x-x_0)$ is equal to zero when $x$ is not equal to $x_0$. $$\delta(x-x_{0})=0, \text{when } x \neq x_0$$

> [!NOTE] **Property 2**
>  The integral of the Dirac delta function within the interval is equal to 1. $$\int_{x_{0}-\epsilon}^{x_{0}-\epsilon} \delta(x-x_{0)}dx =1, \text{when } \epsilon>0$$
