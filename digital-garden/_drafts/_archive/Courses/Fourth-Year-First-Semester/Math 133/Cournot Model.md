Cournot competition is an economic model describing *an industry structure in which rival companies offering an identical product compete on the amount of output they produce, independently and at the same time.* It is named after its founder, French mathematician Augustin Cournot.

### Assumptions of the Model
- **There are two competing firms** procusing a single homogenous product.
- The **firms must chose how much of the good to produce.**
- The **cost of producing one unit** of the good **is a constant** $c$, and is the same in both firms.
- **There is no setup cost.** If a Firm $i$ produces the quantity $q_i$ units of the good, then the cost to Firm $i$ is $cq_i$, for $i=1,2$.


### The Price Function
The price of a unit of the good is **negatively related** to the total amount produced. If Firm 1 produces $q_1$ and Firm 2 produces $q_2$ for a total of $Q = q_{1}+ q_{2}$, then we have the price function:

In the price function, the quantity $a$ is the **total market demand**.

Assume that the firms chooose their production quantities simultaneously and each firm chooses some production quantity $0\leq q_{i}\leq a$.

### Profit
The profit of each firm is equal to the **Revenue** (Quantity x Market Price) less the **Production Cost** (Quantity x Cost of Production).


![[Pasted image 20211201193114.png]]

For the firms to realize profit, we assume that the demand $a$ is greater than the cost $c$.


### Monopoly Production

First consider the case of a monopoly - where there is only one producer. Thus $q_{2}= 0$. Then the return to Firm 1 if it produces $q_1$ units is:
$$u(q_{1)}= q_{1}(a-q_{1})- cq_1$$


Since there is no competition, the firm will choose to maximize the quantity $q_1$. This model is advantageous to the company but not to the consumers.

In a Cournot Monopoly Model, we have the following solution:

- Production: $q_{1}= \frac{a-c}{2}$
- Price: $P(\frac{a-c}{2})=\frac{a-c}{2}$
- Profit: $u\left(\frac{a-c}{2}\right)= \frac{(a-c)^2}{4}$


### Duopoly Production
In a Cournot Duopoly Model, we have the following solution:


- Production: $q_{1}= q_{2}= \frac{a-c}{3}$
- Profit: $u_{1}= u_{2}= \frac{(a-c)^2}{9}$
- Price: $P(\frac{2(a-c)}{3})= \frac{a+2c}{3}$
