One of my primary motivation in learning 日本語 is my interest in anime and Japanese pop music. Around the end of March, I started watching Black Clover. One of my favorite songs from that anime is 『Black Catcher』 by Vickeblanka which, I played over and over - not skipping any OP - so I kinda know the lyrics without really knowing what it means. It frustrates me when I can feel the emotion of the song but cannot feel the connection to its lyrics. So here's my story (and shocking revelations) as I try to understand the last few lines of the song 『Black Catcher』

So the first revelation to me was revealed when we started discussing the て-form conjugation of verbs and how it is used in describing a series of actions. This is the first time I had an idea of the last lines of the song which has a lot of て-form verbs:

ほらみかたにかえて、
そのみかたをかえて、
そしてみらいをかえて、
We can catch the game

So I looked up the dictionary for かえて - it is the て-form of 変える（かえる）which means "to change", "to transform" or "to turn (into)". Do not confuse it with the verb that we know: 帰る（かえる - to return）which is an u-verb. Here,  変える（かえる）is a ru-verb.

At this point I only have a vague idea of the song: "changing みかた, then changing the future, so that we can catch the game"

The second shocking revelation occured to me as I was looking up the meaning of みかた. At first I thought that the song speaks of the same object. It turns out the two instances of みかた stands for two different nouns! In the first line, it means "ally/friend" while on the second line, it means "a perspective/way of seeing things". 

Let's also notice the use of different particles in the first two lines even though they use the same verb. Why did the composer used the particle に in the first line instead of を? What's the difference? Is みかた not a direct object of the verb 変える? 

Turns out --- it is not.

-------------------------------------------------

We know that we can use に to specify a literal destination - as in a place. It turns out, に can also be used to mean metaphorical destination - as in a result/direction of an action.

With the verb 変える, whatever you mark with を is the thing that gets changed, and whatever you mark with に is what it changes into.

For example:
かばんをかえて (change the bag)
かばんにかえて  (turn into a bag)

So for instance, we can form the sentence:
Plasticをかばんにかえて  (Turn the plastic into a bag.)

-------------------------------------------------

Going back to the song, it was just at this moment that I realize the importance of the lines that preceded it - as it forms a complete sentence. 

But before that, we need a little more vocabulary to completely understand the stanza:

めにうつった - conjugated form of the verb めにうつる which means "to be visible"
すべて - means "everything"
ほら - an interjection, meaning "look!"

-------------------------------------------------

So the lines of the song are:

まけないでよ。
めにうつったすべてを
ほらみかたにかえて、
そのみかたをかえて、
そしてみらいをかえて、
we can catch the game。

Here, めにうつったすべてをほらみかたにかえて loosely translates to "Look! Everything you see, turn it into an ally."

So that the whole thing translates to

Don't lose.
Look! Everything you see,
turn it into an ally, 
then change this perspective, 
and then change the future, 
we can catch this game.

Understanding even just this part of the song adds another layer of meaning to it. Of course, you can just look up the english translation of the lyrics but its still it hits different when you sing it with words you really understand. I tend to appreciate it even more. It is a song about not giving up and about changing the world -  a fitting song for a boy who doesn't know when to quit, don't you think?
