#### 1. て Form Conjugation of Verbs 

**Why do we have a て-form conjugation?**
- making requests ("...please...")
- giving and asking for permission("You may ...."/"May I ...")
- stating that something is forbidden ("You must not ...")
- forming a sentence that describes two or more events ("I did this and did that")
<!--ID: 1645617151820-->


So how do we conjugate verbs into て-form?

**For ru-verbs:**

> Take る off and then add て
> Example: たべる -> たべて

**For u-verbs, there are 5 rules. All of which depends on the verb's last syllable.**

> 1. last syllable: う、つ、る
> - あう -> あって
> - まつ -> まって
> - とる -> とって

> 2. last syllable: む、ぶ、ぬ
> - よむ -> よんで
> - あそぶ -> あそんで
> - しぬ -> しんで


> 3. last syllable: く
> - かく -> かいて
> Exception: いく -> いって


> 4. last syllable: ぐ
> - およぐ -> およいで


> 5. last syllable: す
> - はなす -> はなして


**For irregular verbs:**

> irregular verbs
> - する -> して
> - くる -> きて

#### 2. ～てください
**To make a polite request** from another person, use the te-form of the verb + ください.
<!--ID: 1645617151826-->


> まってください
> Please wait.

> かいてくださ
> Please write.

---
#### 3. ～てもいいです　(Permission function)
Using the te-form of a verb + もいいです means **"you may do"**. It means you are **giving permission to perform an action**.
<!--ID: 1645617151833-->


- **To ask for permission**, you can turn it into a question ～てもいいですか
- **In casual conversations, you can drop the も**, and say for example, たべていいです。In contrast, the particle は in prohibition cannot be dropped.

Some Examples:
> ここでたばこをすってもいいですか (May I smoke here?)
> たばこをすってはいけません。(Smoking is prohibited)

> あそこでたべてもいいですか (May I eat over there?)
> あそこでたべてもいいです (You may eat over there)
> - Alternative: どうそ、たべてくださ (go on, please do(eat) so)

> それはしゃしんをとてもいいですか。(Can I take a picture of that?)
> どうそ、とてください　(Go on, please do)

---
#### 4. ~てはいけません (Prohibition function)
A verbal te-form plus ～はいけません means "y**ou must not do**..." a **strong prohibition statement, as in rules and regulations.**
- Pronounced as: tewaikemasen
- は there is the particle は(wa).This particle cannot be dropped even in casual conversations.
- いけません means "cannot go do (the subject - the verb)"
	- いけ is the "can do"-form of the verb いく (to go).
- Using this form may sound too harsh if you are not a person of authority.
- Be careful to only use this when there are definite rules in place.
<!--ID: 1645617151839-->


Appropriation: You use ~てはいけません for **prohibiting actions**.

#### 5. Describing two or more activities
- You can use the te-form of the verb if you want to combine two or more verbs, as in describing a sequence of events or actions.
<!--ID: 1645617151846-->


Example
> ノートをかりて、コピーします。
> I will borrow her notebook and photocopy it.

> 今日は、ろくじにおきて、べんきょうしました。
> Today, I got up at six, and studied.

- The te-form of the verb can also be used to loosly describe the rest of the sentence. As in describing how the second verb was performed, or describing the situation.

Some Examples:
> バスにのって、かいしゃに行きます。
> I go to work (on a bus)

> きょかしょうをわすれて、すみません
> I'm sorry (I forgot my textbook)

#### 6. ～から
A sentence that ends with から(because) explains the reason or the cause of a situation, a proposal and so forth.
<!--ID: 1645617151852-->


Format:
> (situation)。 (explanation)から。

Some examples:
> 私はこんばんべんきょうします。あしたテストをありますから。
> I will study this evening. Because we have an exam tomorrow.

> バスにのりましょう。タクシーはたかいですから。
> Let's go by bus. (Because) taxis are expensive.

- The explanation clause may also preede the clause. Thus the first example can be phrased as:

> あしたテストがありますから、私はこんばんべんきょうします。
> I will study this evening. (Because) we have an exam tomorrow.


---
#### 7. ～ましょうか
We know that we can use ～ましょうか to **invite people to do some action**, as in "Let's..." or "Shall we ...?"
<!--ID: 1645617151859-->


**Another use** of ～ましょうか is **to offer help**, as in the sense "let me do ...".

If you see a person having a hard time doing something, you can say:

> やりましょうあ。
> I'll do it/ Let me do it.

Or to a person who is carrying a heavy bag:

> にもつをもちましょうか。
> Shall I carry your bag?

- おそく／おそい 。おそく and おそい both mean "late" but are used differently since おそく is an adverb while おそい is an adjective.
	- おそい modifies a noun
<!--ID: 1645617151865-->


	> おまえはおそい。
	> You are late.
	- おそく modifies verbs
	
	> きのう、おそくねました
	> I sleep late yesterday.

- 　どうも is normally used with ありがと and すみません as in どうもありがとう／どうもすみません。*(Thank you very much/ I am very sorry)*.  When used alone, it is an abbreviation of どうもありがとう／どうもすみません. So you can just say どうも to say thank you instead of a long sentence. Some people use どうも as *"hello"* or as *"goodbye"*.

- Many words that begin with お can be used without it.The use of お in such words simply adds smoothness and nuance of social refinement, without changing the meaning of words.

	- Example:
		- おかね can simply be かね
		- おさけ can simply be さけ
