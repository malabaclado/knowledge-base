Recall that if $\alpha$ is algebraic over F, then there exists $f(x) \in F[x]$ such that $f(\alpha)=0$. If this is the case, then there are infinitely many other polynomials over F having $\alpha$ as a zero. Among all these polynomials, the next theorem guarantees that there exists an irreducible one which generates all the others.

> Theorem. (**Existence of an Irreducible Polynomial**)
> Suppose $E$ is an extension field of a field $F$ and $\alpha \in E$. Then there exists an irreducible polynomial $p(x) \in F[x]$ such that $p(\alpha)=0$. Moreover, if $f(x)\in F[x]$ such that $f(\alpha)=0$, then $p(x)|f(x)$. This polynomial is unique up to a constant factor and can always be chosen as monic.

#### Proof of Theorem

**Existence of the $p(x)$ by construction**
Let $\phi_{\alpha}$ be the evaluation homomorphism of $F[x]$ into $E$. Then the kernel is an ideal an by theorem^[Thm 27.24 in Fraleigh], it is a principal ideal generated by some element $p(x)$ in $F[x]$ so that: $$\ker \phi_{\alpha}=\langle p(x) \rangle$$

The set $\langle p(x) \rangle$ contains precisely the elements having $\alpha$ as a zero. 

Let $f(x)$ be a nonzero polynomial such that $f(\alpha)=0$. Then $f(x) \in \langle p(x) \rangle$ which implies that $p(x)|f(x)$. Hence we have $p(x)$ a polynomial of minimal degree$\geq 1$ in $F[x]$ having $\alpha$ as zero.

**Irreducibility of $p(x)$**

Now we show that $p(x)$ is an irreducible polynomial. We prove this *by contradiction*.

Suppose we have the factorization $p(x)=r(x)s(x)$ where $$0 \leq \deg r, \deg s \leq \deg p$$
Since $p(\alpha)=0$ then we have 

$$r(\alpha)s(\alpha)=0$$
 
 and since E is a field (thus having no zero divisors), then either $r(\alpha)=0$ or $s(\alpha)=0$ which is a contradiction since we know that $p(x)$ is the lowest degree polynomial that have $\alpha$ as zero. Thus, it is the case that $p(x)$ is irreducible.

**p(x) can always be chosen as monic**

Suppose $p(x)$ is a polynomial of the form:
$$p(x)=a_nx^n+...+a_0$$
with $a_n\neq 0$

If we divide $p(x)$ by $a_n$, we still have 
$$\frac{p(\alpha)}{a_n}=0$$

which is a monic polynomial. 

We can always choose this to be our irreducible polynomial.
#### Some Examples
1.	$\sqrt{2}\in \mathbb{R}$ is algebraic over $\mathbb{Q}$

**To find the irreducible polynomial:**
Let $\alpha=\sqrt{2}$.

$$\begin{align}
\alpha &=\sqrt{2}\\
\alpha^2 &= 2 \\
\alpha^2 -2 =0
\end{align}$$

Thus we have $irr(\sqrt{2},\mathbb{Q})=x^2-2$ and $\deg(\sqrt{2},\mathbb{Q})=2$.

2. $i \in \mathbb{C}$ is algebraic over $\mathbb{R}$

**To find the irreducible polynomial:**
Let $\alpha=i$

$$\begin{align}
\alpha &=i \\
\alpha^2 &= -1\\
\alpha^2+1 &= 0
\end{align}$$

Thus we have $irr(i,\mathbb{C})=x^2+1$ and $\deg(i,\mathbb{C})=2$.
