First note that $8=2^3$.

We can find this field by extending $\mathbb{Z}_2$.

So we'll find an irreducible polynomial of degree 3 in $\mathbb{Z}_2$.

Consider $p(x)=x^3+x+1$. This has no zero in $\mathbb{Z}_2$, hence $p(x)$ is irreducible.

Let $\alpha$ be a zero of $p(x)$ in some extension $E$ of $\mathbb{Z}_2$.  Thus $\alpha$ is algebraic over $\mathbb{Z}_2$ and $$irr(\alpha,\mathbb{Z}_2)=p(x)=x^3+x+1$$

By theorem, we can form $\mathbb{Z}_2$ with elements $$a+b\alpha+c\alpha^2,\quad a,b,c \in \mathbb{Z}_2$$

So we have $|\mathbb{Z}_2(\alpha)|=8$

Some other observation:

- $\langle \mathbb{F}_8, + \rangle \cong \mathbb{Z}_2 \times \mathbb{Z}_2 \times\mathbb{Z}_2$
- $\langle \mathbb{F}_8, \cdot \rangle \cong \mathbb{Z}_7$
