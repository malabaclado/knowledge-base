**Before you write:**
- Who are you writing for? 
	- People who have no background on your topic.
		- Assume people are interested on the topic.
		- Assume they already completed their bs math program
	- Adviser/Examiner

-	What is your goal in writing?
	-	To be able to explain the topic
	-	To have the reader appreciate your topic

### Organization

**Three Levels of Organization**


1. General structure of the whole thesis
	- identify title, chapters, sections

2. Individual paragraphs and proofs
3. Writing sentences

---

**A thesis should tell a story.**
- Title and Abstract 
	- abstract :: movie trailer
- Introduction to the topic
	- setting the stage for the discussion
	- should be digestible, do not use heavy terminologies
- Background and Prelimenaries
	- related topics
- Analysis/Results
	- your own work
- Optional: Conclusion/Open Problems in this topic
	- final remarks; may use heavy references
- References



---
**Title**
- Title should be clear and concise
- Not to long (5 to 20 words on average)
- short but descriptive


**Abstract**
- very short summary

**Introduction**
- statement of the problem
- historical background
- connection to other problems in the field and **other relevant work**
- you should be able to answer: *"what's the motivation for this research?"*
- give **outline or roadmap** of the whole paper (in paragraph form)
- **this is the part where you sell your thesis**


**Background and Prelimenaries**
- discussion of all material not covered in the BS Math curriculum
- formal definitions and theorems
- only use definitions and theorems that **you actually use in the thesis**



**Analysis and Result**
- may be divided into 2 or more section or chapters
- section/chapter titles should reflect content
- begin each chapter with short paragraph summarizing content
- add transition paragraphs between sections
- give appropriate examples


**References**
- include only references cited in the thesis
- use correct bibliographic formatting

### Good Mathematical Writing
Characteristics of a Good Mathematical Writing
- Precise
	- use the right words
- Clear
	- avoid vagueness
	- no unnecessary grammatical structures
- Concise 
	- simple and  straight to the point
- Familar
	- use familar language
- Fortright
	- being honest,and friendly
	- do not use cringy superlative
- Fluid
	- being smooth in transitions


**Do's and Don'ts**
- use clear, simple language
- highlight structure: explain what you are doing and why you are doing it
	- *give lots of spoilers!*
- Provide insights and motivation
