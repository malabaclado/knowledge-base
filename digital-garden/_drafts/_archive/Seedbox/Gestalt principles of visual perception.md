Developed by German psychologists, the **Gestalt** **principles**, also known as the **Gestalt** laws of perceptual organization, describe how we interpret the complex world around us. 

### Proximity
![[Pasted image 20230906001212.png]]

The brain categorizes things close together as being part of a group. In the right pane, difference in spaces helps you easily identify groupings by columns or by rows.

### Similarity
![[Pasted image 20230906001406.png]]

We tend to identify similar things (color, shape, sizes) as related.

### Enclosure
![[Pasted image 20230906001502.png]]
We tend to differentiate between inside and outside. Objects inside an enclosure are usually related.

### Closure
![[Pasted image 20230906001559.png]]
We tend to see the whole picture than the individual parts. For example, we tend to identify the circle first before the individual parts of it. 

How to apply it: in graphs with borders, the first thing people see is the borders. Remove borders if we want the data to stand out.

![[Pasted image 20230906001804.png]]
We tend to identify and follow visual pattern. In the left pane, we usually visualize 2 first rather than 3 as part of a pattern that makes sense to us logically.

How to use it: Use it to strip out unnecessary details (such as border and axis lines) when you have an ordered data or axis labels.

### Connection
![[Pasted image 20230906002026.png]]
We tend to identify connected things as related.

How to use it: Use it to show movement or growth.
