# Module 2 -Position of the Self in Relation to Public Speaking
> Do you have what it takes to speak up and defend your ideas?

In this module, you will be reflecting on factors that contribute to your self concept, self awareness, and identity. Hopefully, you will recognize how these affect how and what you communicate, especially in front of an audience.



**Self-concept**
- Self-concept is defined as the “sum total of knowledge that you have about yourself”^[[[Solomon & Theiss(2013), pp. 70-82]]]
- It is characterized as being subjective, multifaceted, and dynamic.
- self is a product of our communication with others, which she groups into two^[[[Wood(2016) - Communication and Personal Identity]]]: 
	- particular others: family, friends, etc
	- generalized other: society, other people, "kapwa"

**Identity**
- “identity is the image of yourself that is embodied in communication”^[Solomon and Theiss (2013), p. 79]
- Just as the self is multifaceted, identity is multilayered.
	- personal layer - self perceptions
	- enactment layer - personal characteristics thru verbal/nonverbal messages
	- relational layer: shaped by personal relationships
	- communal layer: how social groups affect identity

**The Filipino Self: Kapwa**
- Virgilio Enriquez, the father of Sikolohiyang Pilipino proposed kapwa as the core concept explaining interpersonal behavior emphasizing the self as fundamentally related to others and not separate or distinct as compared to the West.
- Having a collectivist culture, our country considers the family as the prototype of all social relationships.
- Other Filipino communication concepts: pakikiramdam, pakikipagkapwa

**Self-awareness**
> The self is dynamic, ever-changing, and always a work-in-progress. We always find ourselves in situations which enable us to know ourselves better, cultivate our strengths, and work on our weaknesses.

**Public Speaking Anxiety**
- The fear of public speaking is called glossophobia.
- public speaking anxiety affects at least 70% of the world’s population^[(Montopoli, 2017)]
- A study conducted by Del Villar (2010) among Communication 3 (the predecessor of Speech 30) students in UP Diliman revealed that they attributed their public speaking anxiety to the following
	- Expectations (as a speaker)
	- Training & Exposure (lack of)
	- Audience
	- Self-valuation (looks and experience)
	- Rejection 
	- Verbal fluency (ot being to say their thoughts)
	- Preparation (lack of)
	- Previous unpleasant experience

**Communication Apprehension**
- an individual’s level of fear or anxiety associated with either real or anticipated communication with another person or persons^[McCroskey(1984), (p. 13).]
- There are two types of communication apprehension
	- Situational (State)
	- Trait (Traitlike)
- Situational anxiety is “cause by factors in a specific situation”^[(Hamilton, 2012, p. 28)]
