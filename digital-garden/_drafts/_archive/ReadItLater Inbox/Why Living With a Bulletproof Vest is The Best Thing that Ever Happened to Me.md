# [Why Living With a Bulletproof Vest is The Best Thing that Ever Happened to Me](https://existentialespresso.substack.com/p/why-living-with-a-bulletproof-vest)

> "Every difficulty in life presents us with an opportunity to turn inward and to invoke our own submerged inner resources. The trials we endure can and should introduce us to our strengths. " – Epictetus

This time, I want to talk about a difficulty, a burden, that seemed too much for me to bear. Something that, at first, I viewed as a symbol of everything that went wrong in my life. A symbol of my life being a disaster. But it ended up being the best thing that happened to me.

And I want to make a point that no matter how big or how small, you can, and should, use the difficulties in your life to strengthen yourself.

Okay, first of all, living with a bulletproof vest is not really the best thing that ever happened to me.

But, believe it or not, it is one of the best.

I lived with a bulletproof vest for almost 2 years.

To be more precise, from April 2020 to the end of January 2022, every time I left my house I had to wear a bulletproof vest. It was an essential part of my outfit.

If you have no idea why I would have to wear it, read **["Why Having A Price On My Head Didn't Upset Me"](https://existentialespresso.substack.com/p/why-having-a-price-on-my-head-didnt)** for some context.

Wearing a bulletproof vest is extremely uncomfortable.

In order for it not to be easily seen under your clothes (and you definitely don’t want that), it has to be tightened up really good.

When you tighten it up, the vest presses on your chest and your whole abdomen.

What happens next is, you get hot. You start sweating. A lot. It doesn’t matter if it's the middle of summer or if it’s the coldest winter day, you are soaking wet under your shirt.

However, are you sweating because of the vest, because of that thick additional layer of clothing?

Or are you sweating because of the very fact that you need to wear a bulletproof vest?

Honestly, after so much time spent wearing it, I still haven’t figured that out. It’s probably both.

The second thing is that you are not breathing normally. Your chest and your abdomen are not in their normal state. They are not relaxed. They are being pressed on. Your breathing becomes shallow.

Are you breathing heavily just because your chest and abdomen are being pressed on?

Or are you also breathing heavily because you are hot from that thick additional layer of clothing?

Or, are you breathing heavily because of the fact that you have to wear a bulletproof vest?

I don’t know. It’s probably all of those things.

Then, especially in an outdoor environment where there are a lot of strangers around you, you get a rush of anxiety.

Is it because of the physiological factors? Because your body temperature is high, you are sweating, and your breathing is shallow and rapid?

Or are you having anxiety because of the fact that you need to wear a bulletproof vest every time you leave your house, and you need to pay attention to all the unknown faces around you?

I don’t know. It’s probably both.

However, it’s not just the vest in a physical sense.

It’s not just that you are wearing this unusual piece of clothing in order to protect yourself when you are outside.

It’s not just the physical burden, discomfort, and pressure.

You are also wearing that same vest in a symbolical sense. It’s more than physical. I will allow myself to say that it’s metaphysical.

In physical reality, the vest doesn’t allow you to breathe normally, you cannot even sit comfortably, you cannot hug anyone so that they don’t figure out that you are wearing it. You simply cannot breathe, move, or behave like a normal human being. However, that’s not what’s truly difficult about it.

On a symbolical, metaphysical level, the burden is even heavier. It’s not just that you cannot breathe or move normally, you cannot live normally. It’s not just your chest and abdomen that are being pressed on. Your life, in the deepest sense of the word, is being pressed on.

**Your soul cannot breathe at its full capacity.**

So how could this be one of the best things that ever happened to me?

Before I started wearing a bulletproof vest, I already had a couple of years of experience in training with a weighted vest.

It is used to create additional resistance when you are doing bodyweight exercises. If you’ve ever used it, you know that it’s an amazing training tool.

Spend a period of time training with a weighted vest. Use it whenever you are doing squats, push ups, pull ups, burpees. Take some time, get used to it. Make it your new normal.

Then, one day, remove that weighted vest. Do a workout consisting of the same movements you’ve been doing, but this time without the additional resistance.

I don’t know if you are an anime fan, but you are going to feel like Goku after removing his weighted clothes, or Rock Lee after removing weights from his legs.

And that’s what I actually did when I got my weighted vest. I was training at home, so almost all I did for a period of time was squats, pull ups and push ups with a weighted vest.

I knew that feeling of removing the vest and feeling like a superhuman version of yourself.

I would be lying if I said that, from the moment I started wearing the bulletproof vest, I created this analogy in my head that the bulletproof vest is actually a symbolical weighted vest that is only going to make me stronger.

No, it was the complete opposite. Every time I was getting ready to leave the house and was putting it on, I got angry.

I was angry at life for putting me in this situation.

I was angry that other people could live normally but I couldn’t.

How I overcame this anger is a topic for another essay, or even a series of essays.

**But I did overcome it.**

I accepted and got used to both the physical and symbolical burden of wearing a bulletproof vest.

Then, one day, I suddenly thought:  
 **"Imagine when I remove this vest. My soul and my mind will probably feel just like my body feels when I remove the weighted vest. "**

And they did.

In January 2022, when I embarked on this adventure of engaging with the world once again, of reclaiming my life and my fate, I had my Goku and Rock Lee moment.

For the first couple of days, everything was too overwhelming for me to understand what was happening.

I left my home, my job, the life that I was building, and all the people that I loved and cared about, and went to the other side of the world.

I was nervous, excited, anxious, joyful, happy, sad, hyped up, scared, everything. All of the emotions were too intense those first couple of days for me to be fully aware of my situation.

Then, one day, as I was walking down the street, it hit me.

**I am not wearing a vest anymore.**

I am able to breathe normally. I am able to move normally. Like a normal human being, like a free human being.

I don’t have to look behind my back every couple of meters.

I don’t have to be worried when there is a car or a bike passing by me.

I don’t have to be alert at all times. I can get lost in my thoughts while I look at the world around me.

I am still sweating as much as I did before, but now at least I know that it’s because of the tropical climate.

I am not nervous, anxious, or angry because I’m sweating. I am smiling.

Some resistance has been taken off.

**My soul is able to breathe once again.**

If I was able to stay strong and develop a healthy attitude towards life while I was wearing that vest, it is only right that now I go beyond, that I embrace life and engage in it with even greater intensity.

And that’s what I did.

That was, without a doubt, one of the greatest moments of my life.

However, at that point in my life, just like at the moment of writing this, my life is far from being free of burden, obstacles, and symbolical weight. But I am stronger than I’ve ever been. And I am strong precisely because of the burden I’ve been carrying, the resistance I’ve been living with.

To put it metaphorically, life is still making me do burpees every single day. Some days it's more than I think I can handle. But now I’m doing them without the vest, without the additional resistance. And if there is still some, or even a lot of additional resistance, the resistance that has been removed is so great that I cannot help but be stronger.

**Make no mistake, you have your vest.**

**Recognize it. Learn to appreciate it.**

It’s only if you recognize it and take it for what it is that you will be able to feel and appreciate your strength once the weight has been removed.

And this is not something that’s applicable only to life’s extreme situations, like this one I’ve shared with you.

I’ve been using this same philosophy for multiple different, much smaller things since I’ve got rid of my vest.

At the beginning of the year, I moved to a country where, for 2.5 months, there were daily power cuts, ranging from 4 hours per day to 12 hours per day.

Obviously, it affected my work. It also affected my training and my daily routine as a whole.  
(*shout out to me for staying consistent with my instagram content during this period)  
*  
I identified this as another resistance that will eventually be removed. "And once it is removed," I said to myself, "I’ll go into turbo mode."

And I did. I started writing more than ever. I improved my online training business. I re-established a healthy and enjoyable daily routine.

Recently, I was without my laptop for a week, so I had to do all of my work over the phone. Seemingly insignificant, it definitely cannot be compared to wearing a bulletproof vest. But still, resistance. Resistance that will eventually be removed.

"And once it is removed, I will, again, go to a higher level. In this case, just a higher level of writing. If I was able to write this much on my phone, I can do at least 50% more on my laptop. "

I had that in mind, and I just waited to take advantage of it when I got my laptop back. 

So, once again, I invite you to recognize your vest.

No matter how light or how heavy it is. Make use of it.

To be fair, I don’t know when your vest will be removed. I cannot even guarantee that it will.

But I am sure about one thing:

**If you view it just as a thing that is making your life worse, and nothing more, you can’t expect to feel stronger once it has been removed.**
