In statistics, a [[mixture model]] is a [[probabilistic model]] for representing the presence of subpopulations within an overall population, without requiring that an observed data set should identify the sub-population to which an individual observation belongs. Formally a mixture model corresponds to the mixture distribution that represents the probability distribution of observations in the overall population. However, while problems associated with "mixture distributions" relate to deriving the properties of the overall population from those of the sub-populations, "mixture models" are used to make statistical inferences about the properties of the sub-populations given only observations on the pooled population, without sub-population identity information.

A [[Gaussian Mixture Model]] (GMM) is a clustering technique employed in machine learning. It operates as a soft-clustering method, meaning it assigns data points to multiple clusters with varying degrees of membership probabilities. The process involves constructing a Gaussian Mixture, and the Expectation-Maximization (EM) algorithm is then applied to determine the optimal parameters for the model. This method is particularly useful for capturing complex data distributions and handling situations where a data point may belong to more than one cluster.
