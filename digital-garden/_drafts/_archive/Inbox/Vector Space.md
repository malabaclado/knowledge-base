# Definition
A vector space V is a set that is closed under finite vector addition and scalar multiplication. 

For a general vector space, the scalars are members of a field $\mathbb{F}$, in which case $V$ is called a vector space over $\mathbb{F}$.

The Euclidean n-space $\mathbb{R}^{n}$ is called a [[real vector space]], and $\mathbb{C}^{n}$ is called a [[complex vector space]].

# Properties
In order for $V$ to be a vector space, the following conditions must hold for all elements in $V$ and scalars in $\mathbb{F}$.
1. Commutativity
2. Associativity of vector addition
3. Additive identity
4. Existence of additive inverse
5. Associativity of scalar multiplication
6. Distributivity of scalar sums
7. Distributivity of vector sums
8. Scalar multiplication identity

# Example
- Let $V=\mathbb{F}^{n}$, the set of all ordered n-tuples of elements of a field $\mathbb{F}$ with addition and scalar multiplication defined as:
$$\begin{align}
(a_{1}, a_{2}, \dots, a_{n} ) + (b_{1},b_{2},\dots,b_{n}) &= (a_{1}+b_{1}, a_{2}+b_{2} + ,\dots, a_{n}+b_{n}) \\
c(a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{n}) &= (ca_{1},ca_{2},\dots,ca_{n}) \\
\end{align}$$

# Important Remarks
- The zero vector is unique
- The additive inverse of any vector is unique
- $0v=0_{V}$ for any $v\in V$ , where $0$ is the zero in the field
- $(-1)v=-v$ for any $v\in V$
- If $c\in \mathbb{F}$ and $v\in V$ such that $cv=0_{V}$, then $cv=0_{V}$ or $v=0_{V}$ ^9j9sc8
