Psychological resistance is a concept I've encountered from the sales book [[Objections]]. 

It is the reason why you cannot win argument by showing people that they are wrong - because the tendency is to defend themselves and win the argument.

In the book, Jeb Blount describes this:

> Overcoming doesn't work. It has never really worked. You cannot argue your stakeholders into believing that they are wrong. The more you push, the more they'll dig their heels in and resist you. This behavior is called _psychological reactance._
> 
> ...
> 
> The act of overcoming creates animosity, exasperation, and frustration for both the stakeholders, who get bulldozed with an argument about why they are wrong, and the salesperson, who creates even more resistance and harsher rejection with this approach. So stakeholders cloud the issues, become stubborn and illogical, dig their heels in, and even lie.
