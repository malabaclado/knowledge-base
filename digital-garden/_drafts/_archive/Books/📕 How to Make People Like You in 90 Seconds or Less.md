## Three stages when meeting new people 
1. meeting 
	- *"If you make the right impression during the first 3-4 seconds of a new meeting, you can create an awareness that you are sincere, safe, trustworthy and the opportunity to go further and create rapport witll present itself."
2. establishing rapport 
3. communicating


> If meeting is the physical comming together of two or more people, then communicating is what we do from the moment we are fully aware of another's presence. And between these two events -- meeting and communicating -- lies the 90-second land of rapport that links them together.

## Five parts to a successful greeting: Open-Eye-Beam-Hi!-Lean
*Have you ever met someone and immediately realize: "Wow, I like this person!"*
- Open. Open your attitude and your body.
	- Face the person your meeting. Keep your heart/chest directly aimed at them. Your body should be screaming out: *"I'm focusing on you"*.
- Eye. Be the first to initiate eye contact.
	- Practice tip: When watching movies, identify the color of the character's eyes and keep them to yourself. Do the same practice when talking to other people.
- Beam. Give out a smile.
- Hi!
	- Say hi with a warm tone. 
	- Mention your name. This is your first opportunity to make them aware of your presence.
	- Extend your hand to the person.
	- If they mention their name, find a convenient way to say their names three times to commit it to memory.
- Lean. 
	- Subtle forward tilt. This indicates interest and openness to the person you are talking to.

## On handshakes
- Certain expectations accompany a handshake. It should be firm and respectful. 
	- Deviate from this expectation and the other person will feel something is wrong. The brain hates confusion and when faced with it, the first instinct is to withdraw.
- You can do a hands-free handshake.
	- Just do everythin you would do but without using your hands.
	- Point your heart to the other person and say hello. Light up your eyes and smile, give off the same energy that usually accompanies the full-blown handshake.

## On establishing rapport
*If meeting is the physical comming together of two or more people, then communicating is what we do from the moment we are fully aware of another's presence. And between these two events -- meeting and communicating -- lies the 90-second land of rapport that links them together.*

- Rapport is the establishment of common ground, of a comfort zone where two or more people can mentally join together.
- Rapport allows social exchanges to flow smoothly.
- The prize when you achieve rapport is the other person's acceptance.
	- When you've build rapport, you've passed the stage of *"Can I trust this person?"* and allows the conversation to focus on your message.
- Sometimes, rapport happen by itself. Sometimes, you need to give it a helping hand.
- Your ability to establish rapport will depend on four things:
	- Your attitude
	- Your body language and voice tone
	- Your conversation skills
	- Your ability to discover which sense the other person relies on the most

	## Three distinct parts of effective communication
*"The meaning of communication lies in the response it gets."* - Richard Bandler and John Grinder

- The formula of affective communication has three parts:
	- Know what you want. 
		- Formulate your intention in the affirmative and present tense
			- ❌ *"I don't want to be lonely."*
			- ✔️ *"I want a successful relationship with the people a round me."
	- Find out what you're getting.
		- Assess what you're doing to achieve your goal.
	- Change what you do until you get what you want.
		- Design a plan and go through with it. Do it and get feedback. 
		- Redesign your plan as necessary, do it again and evaluate after.
		- Repeat the cycle until you achieve your goal.
		- This tip is applicable to any are of your life that you want to improve - finance, romance, sports, career, you name it.
