# First Night: Deny Trauma  
The philosopher posits that everyone can change and be happy.  
  
Trauma is not real. As Adler said:  
  
> *“No experience is in itself a cause of our success or failure. We do not suffer from the shock of our experiences—the so-called trauma—but instead we make out of them whatever suits our purposes. We are not determined by our experiences, but the meaning we give them is self-determining.”*  
  

> [!tips] Actionable Tip
> We should stop making excuses and firmly deny trauma. We are not a product of the past but a form molded by our will to become. Simply put, if you want to do something, overcome all excuses and have the courage to do it.  

  
People fabricate anger in order to show dominance. Anger as an emotion is not a valid excuse to be an asshole.   
  
It is a lie that people do bad things solely because they are angry. When needed, it is not uncommon to see people setting aside their anger. As in the the mother that is angry at her daughter but speaks politely to the teacher.   
  
> In a word, anger is a tool that can be taken out as needed.  
  
- The book does not deny emotion. However it strongly posits that humans are not incapable of resisting emotions.   
- People are not controlled by either their emotion or their past.  
  
In the words of the philosopher:  
  
> *Regardless of what may have happened in the past, it is the meaning that is attributed to it that determines the way someone’s present will be.*  
  
If humans are neither controlled by their emotions nor their past, then it supports the premise that *people can change.*  
  
> *People are not driven by past causes but move toward goals that they themselves set*  
  
The conditions on which someone is born int is not a valid reason for his/her happiness.   
  
> *“The important thing is not what one is born with but what use one makes of that equipment.”*  
  
The philosopher says that being happy is a choice. Likewise, unhappiness is a choice. Envying other people's life thinking you can never be as happy as them is a life-lie. We don't need a replacement, we just need a restart.  
  
> *Does fixating on what you are born with change the reality? We are not replaceable machines. It is not replacement we need but renewal.*  
  
The philosopher introduced what is known in Adlerian philosophy as 'lifestyle'. In his words:  
  
> *How one sees the world. And how one sees oneself. Think of lifestyle as a concept bringing together these ways of finding meaning. In a narrow sense, lifestyle could be defined as someone’s personality; taken more broadly, it is a word that encompasses the worldview of that person and his or her outlook on life.*  
  
Lifestyle is different from our natural sense of personality, that of which cannot be changed.   
  
> *You probably think of disposition or personality as something with which you are endowed, without any connection to your will. In Adlerian psychology, however, lifestyle is thought of as something that you choose for yourself.*  
  
For example, in the book, the youth chose an unhappy lifestyle and goes so far as to choose this was as his personality.  
  
Lifestyles are not something that you are born with. In adlerian psychology, the individual chooses it. If you, even of subconsciously, chose it, then it is possible to choose again.  
  
If lifestyle, such as 'I want to be a happy person' can be chosen (at any given point in time), then why are many people unhappy? The philosopher deeply talks about it in this paragraph:  
  
> *Although there are some small inconveniences and limitations, you probably think that the lifestyle you have now is the most practical one, and that it’s easier to leave things as they are. If you stay just like this, experience enables you to respond properly to events as they occur, while guessing the results of one’s actions. You could say it’s like driving your old, familiar car. It might rattle a bit, but one can take that into account and maneuver easily. On the other hand, if one chooses a new lifestyle, no one can predict what might happen to the new self, or have any idea how to deal with events as they arise. It will be hard to see ahead to the future, and life will be filled with anxiety. A more painful and unhappy life might lie ahead. Simply put, people have various complaints about things, but it’s easier and more secure to be just the way one is.*  
  
In summary, sometimes it is just all to familiar and comfortable not to change. This is equivalent to the new generation's idea of 'settling for less'.  
  
When we try to change our lifestyle, we put out great courage into test. There is anxiety generated by changing but there is disappointment waiting for those who do not. Pick your poison.   
  
One is unable to change because one himself keep repeatedly making the decision not to change.  
  
> *Adler’s teleology tells us, “No matter what has occurred in your life up to this point, it should have no bearing at all on how you live from now on.” That you, living in the here and now, are the one who determines your own life.*
