Red tide is a phenomonon wherein the ocean water changes its color usually but is not limited to red (may be orange, brown, or yellow). This phenomenon is usually accompanied by massive die-offs of marine animals.

Red tides are usually referred by scientists as "harmful algal bloom (HAB's)".

## Why do red tides occur?

Red tides are due to massive increase in population of certain kinds of algae simple plants that live in the sea and freshwater known as **dinoflaggellates**. We call this explosion in population as **algal bloom**.


A number of factors can cause an algal bloom to grow. Here's a few:
- Low salt content in ocean water
- High nutrient content in the water; and
- Warmer-than-usual surface water temperatures 

**Scientists believe that global warming may be contributing to the increase in algal bloom**, resulting in more frequent occurence of red tides.

## What are harmful effects of red tides?

The algae linked to red tides contain a *toxin that affects the nervous and digestive systems of animals*. 


**Humans are also vulnerable to the toxic effects of a red tide.** Due to waves, the algae's toxins may float  into the air, causing respiratory problems among people *near the shoreline*, particularly those with asthma, emphysema or other respiratory illnesses.

Because the toxins can accumulate in shellfish, red tides often cause outbreaks of **parasitic shellfish poisoning (PSP)** and **amnesic shellfish poisoning (ASP)**. Both illnesses affect the nervous systems:
- Effects of ASP: dizziness and disorientation,
- Effects of PSP: respiratory paralysis, resulting in death by asphyxiation.
