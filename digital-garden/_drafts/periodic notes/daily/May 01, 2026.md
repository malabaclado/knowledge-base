- Rare and valuable skills that I don't have right now, but would possibly be good for me
	- Cloud FinOps 
		- While many data professionals can build complex pipelines, very few understand the **unit economics** of the infrastructure they use. Cloud FinOps is the practice of bringing financial accountability to the variable spend of the cloud.
	- Low-level systems programming
		- Most data engineering and data science work happens at a high level of abstraction (Python, SQL). Learning a "systems" language like **Rust** allows you to understand how memory management and CPU cycles actually work.
	- Behavioral Science
		- **Why it’s Rare:** Data engineers and scientists often treat users as rational "data points." Understanding cognitive biases and "nudge" theory allows you to design better data products.
		- **The Value:** You gain the ability to design experiments (A/B testing) that aren't just statistically significant, but psychologically insightful.
	- Technical Writing 
		- The ability to take a complex system and document it so clearly that a junior hire can understand it in ten minutes is a superpower. This is often dismissed as "admin work," but Newport would argue it's a "Remarkability" multiplier (Rule #4).
			- **The Synergy:** Your interest in "Second Brain" systems (Obsidian) already suggests a talent for knowledge organization.
			- **Why it’s Rare:** Most technical documentation is either non-existent or unreadable.
			- **The Value:** If you can create "Internal Documentation as a Product," you become the organizational glue. When a system breaks, people don't just look for the engineer who built it—they look for the person who made it understandable.
