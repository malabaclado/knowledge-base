	-  AI is more likely to transform job descriptions than to eliminate entire professions.
		- **AI helps people be more productive.** Early adopters of AI see significant productivity boosts (up to 72% for some companies).
		- **AI replaces the "shallow" version of professions**—for example, a lawyer who only rewrites templates or a financial advisor who only picks mutual funds from a list.
		- Workers are moving from performing tasks to managing "AI agents" and intelligent systems.
	- **A/N**: So, will AI widen the gap between blue-collar and white-collar jobs? Or not?
	- **Employers are increasingly prioritizing AI skills over years of experience.**
		-  A "skills-first" pathway focuses on specific credentials and hands-on expertise rather than traditional high-cost degrees.
	- **Human intuition remains a critical differentiator.**
		- As AI takes over technical and analytical tasks, human value migrates to "soft skills," which are increasingly recognized as the most difficult to master.
	- **Building a career moat**
		- A **career moat** is a set of rare and valuable skills that provide a competitive advantage and protect long-term employability.
			- **Unattractive but valuable skills**
			- **Early specialization** - Positioning oneself in emerging fields (e.g., Web3 or AI ethics) before they become mainstream.
			- **Skill Stacking** - Being in the top 1% by combining multiple skills (e.g., high EQ + critical thinking + technical AI fluency).
	- **The 3-Bucket Task Analysis**
		- Professionals are encouraged to break their job roles into three categories to determine where to apply AI:
			- **Bucket 1**: Repetitive, rule-based tasks (coding, basic research, summarizing, scheduling) **should be automated**.
			- **Bucket 2:** Tasks done _with_ AI to close experience gaps and increase output (complex drafting, visualization, advanced analytics) (**Augmentation**)
			- **Bucket 3:** Tasks requiring other humans (**Human-only**)

- [How to read a book](https://notebooklm.google.com/notebook/6f4b8c35-dc26-42d4-9fe9-6aafda31ebad)
	- **reading is an active, effortful pursuit aimed at understanding and enlightenment rather than the mere acquisition of information.**
		- To truly possess a book, a reader must move beyond passive consumption to engage in a "conversation" with the author, often through the physical act of marking the text.
		- *reading is not a leisure activity* - the reader must be as active as the writer to successfully receive the intended communication.
