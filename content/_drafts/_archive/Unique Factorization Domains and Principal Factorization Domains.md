## The Ideal Structure in $F[x]$

The **goal** is to construct a field E with F subfield of E, that will contain the zero polynomial $f(x)$ over $F[x]$. 

We discuss here the tools for this goal.


> ==Definition== (Principal Ideal Generated by an Element)
> Let R be a commutative ring with unity. Let $a\in R$. The principal ideal generated by a, denoted $\langle a \rangle$ is defined as $$\langle a \rangle = \{ar|r\in R\}$$


> ==Definition== (Principal Ideal)
> Let I be an ideal of a commutative ring $R$ with unity. We say $I$ is a **principal ideal** of R if there exists $a\in R$ such that $I=\langle a \rangle$

Principal Ideal -> Ideal generated only by a single element in the ring

> ==Theorem==
> Every ideal in F[x] is a principal ideal.

> ==Theorem==
> An ideal $\langle p(x) \rangle \neq \{0\}$ of $F[x]$ is maximal iff $p(x)$ is irreducible over $F[x]$

Remark: Recall in Math 110.1: *Whenever M is a maximal ideal of a commutative ring R with unity, then R/M is a field*. Hence, a maximal ideal in R can be used to construct a field. In particular, if F is a field, then we can form the field $F[x]/ \langle p(x) \rangle$ where $p(x)$ is irreducible over F.

## Unique Factorization Domains

> ==Definition== (Factor)
> Let a and b be elements of an integral domain D. We say **b is a factor of a** iff b divides a (b|a) meaning, there exists c in D such that a=bc.


> ==Definition== (Associates)
> Two nonzero elements a and b in D are called **associates** iff a=ub for some unit u in D.

Remark:
- If $a,b \in D$, then $\langle a \rangle \subseteq \langle b \rangle \Longleftrightarrow b|a$. Moreover, $\langle a \rangle = \langle b \rangle \Longleftrightarrow$ a and b are known associates.

> ==Definition== (Irreducible Element)
> A nonzero element p in an integral domain D that is not a unit is called irreducible if in any factorization $p=ab$, then either a or b is a unit.

> ==Definition== (Unique Factorization Domain)
> An integral domain D is called a unique factorization domain (UID) iff the following conditions are satisfied:
> 1. every nonzero nonunit element of D can be factored into a product of finite number of irreducibles; and 
> 2. if $p_1p_2...p_n$ and $q_1q_2...q_n$ are two factorizations of the same element of D into irreducibles, then r=s and  $q_j$'s can be rearrange so that p_i and q_i are associates for all i.

## Principal Ideal Domains

> ==Definition== (Principal Ideal Domains)
> An integral domain D is called a principal ideal domain iff every ideal in D is a principal ideal.


Example: $\mathbb{Z}$ and $F[x]$ are principal ideal domains.
Nonexample: $\mathbb{Z}[x]$ is a commutative ring that is not a PID

> ==Lemma== (Ascending Chain Condition for a PID)
> Let $D$ be a principal ideal domain. If $N_1 \subseteq N_2 \subseteq ...$ is a monotonic (or strictly monotonic) ascending chain of ideals, then there exists a positive interge r such that $N_r = N_s$ for all $s \geq r$.

> ==Theorem== 
> Let D be a PID. Every element in D that is neither 0 nor a unit is a product of irreducibles.

> ==Lemma==
> Let D be a PID. If $p\in D$, then $\langle p \rangle$ is maximal $\Longleftrightarrow p$ is irreducible.

> ==Lemma==
> If p is irreducible in a PID, then either $p|a$ or $p|b$ whenever p|ab. In general, if p is irreducible and $p|a_1a_2...a_n$, then $p|a_i$ for at least one i.

> ==Definition== (Prime)
> A nonzero nonunit p in an integral domain D with the property that p|ab implies p|a or p|b is a prime. 

> ==Theorem==
> Every PID is a UFD.
