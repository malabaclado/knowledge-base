- # BOND BASICS
	- In bonds, the borrower is the *issuer*, while the lender is the *investor*.
	- ## Bond Issuers
		- Government
		- Quasi-government
		- Supranationals
		- Corporations
	- ## Types of bonds
		- Fixed rates - interest rates doesn't change
		- Floating rates - rates are adjusted periodically depending on a preset formula
	- ## Bond Options (Options are used to protext either the borrower or the lender)
		- Callable bonds
		- Puttable bonds
		- Bond issuance
	- ## Three sources for bond returns
		- Interest rate
		- Curency
		- Credit
	- ## Fundamental Principle in Bond Investing
		- If market interest rates rise, the bond price falls, and market value declines.
		- If the market interest rates falls, bond price rises, and market value increases
		- ![](https://i.imgur.com/lxr7Bvp.png)
		- ![](https://i.imgur.com/XbdMnmv.png)
	- ## On bond duration
		- The shorter the maturity of the bond, the lower is the exposure to price risk, and vice versa.
		- ![](https://i.imgur.com/BkKTTUI.png)
	- ## Risks in Bonds
		- Interest rate risk
		- Inflation risk
		- Credit risk
		- Liquidity risk
		- Exchange risk
	- ## Some factors affecting bond price movement
		- Law of Supply and Demand
		- Economic condition
		- Investors' Confidence
		- Political Environment
	- ## Self-test
		- ![](https://i.imgur.com/jRJzpDp.png)
		- Less tax collection, lest debt payment. Release more bonds at a lower price
		- Decrease. Less crops -> Lest revenue -> Less debts to pay -> More bonds -> Decrease
		- Budget deficit below target -> Better budget/collection -> Increase
		- Gasoline rollback -> Lower revenue for producers -> Less funds to pay debts -> Decrease
		- Country credit rating from BBB to A -> confidence to pay debts increase -> Increase
		- Policy rate from 4.0% to 3.5%  -> Increase
		- Inflation exceeded market estimate -> Higher costs -> Interest rates rise -> Lower market value for bonds
- # EQUITY BASICS
	- Buying stocks is like buying portions of the company.
	- Stocks offer superior returns compared to fixed income instruments, but the risk is also higher.
	- Stocks are offered usually by publicly-listed companies.
	- ## Risks in Investing in Stocks
		- Market risk
		- Liquidity risk
		- Political risk
		- Economic risk
		- Industry risk
	- ## Types of Equities/Stocks
		- Blue-chip stocks - stable, profitable (blue-chips have highest value in casinos)
		- Income stocks  - pays regular dividends (usually companies that provide consumer products)
		- Growth stocks - stocks who's earnings are at above average, possible future blue-chip
		- Defensive stocks - Remain stable at ANY stage of the business cycle (Usually utility stocks eg. Meralco, PLDT)
		- Cyclical Stocks - follows a cycle, sensitive to ecomic structure of the country, best performs in robust economic conditions (eg. ABS-CBN booms during election periods, PH Airlines does good during summer)
		- Speculative stocks - stocks of companies which rise quickly if economy is strong; business operations are not predictive

- # PRULink Funds
	- ## Peso Funds
		- ### Money Market Funds
			- Has lowest risk among all PRULink funds
			- Government bonds, time-deposits
		- *Money market VS Bond fund: Money market invests in shorter duration bonds, Bond fund invests in long duration bonds.*
		- ### Peso bond fund
			- 100% invested in bonds in PH
			- Maximum corporate bond exposure: 20% (There is always at least 80% invested in bonds)
		- ### Cash Flow Fund Hedged Share Class
			- Gives payout of (max 1%) per quarter. Payouts are not guaranteed. 
			- Invests in US high-yield bonds, asian bonds, dividend-yielding equities.
			- This fund is *hedged* to CFF Dollar Fund
			- *How does hedged funds work?*
				- Clients pay in Peso -> Money is converted to Dollar -> Invested in CFF Dollar Cash Flow Fund
				- Exchange rate changes every 30 days
		- ### Managed Fund
			- Invests in both bonds and equities
			- 80% in bonds, 20% in equity
			- Tactical asset allocation (+-20%)
		- ### Proactive Fund
			- 50% Bond, 50% Equity
			- Tactical asset allocation (+-50%)
			- Can go 100% Bonds or 100%
		- ### Growth Fund
			- 20% Bond, 80% Equity
			- Tactical asset allocation (+-20)
		- ### Equity Fund
			- 100% Equities
			- Tactical asset allocation (+-20)
		- ### Equity Index Tracker Fund
			- 100% in PSEI
		- ### Global Equity Navigator Fund
			- Feeder fund - *feeds* from some fund managed by Eastspring
			- Invests on global markets
			- Diversified in terms of sector, country
		- ### Global Market Navigator Fund
			- Multi-asset fund
			- Invested in income and growth generating assets
	- ## Dollar Funds
		- ### Dollar Bond Fund
			- Invested in 100% ROP (Republic of the Philippines)
			- Dollars but invested in PH bonds (PH borrows dollars from you)
		- ### Asian Local Bond Fund
			- Invested in Asian and Local funds (Except Japan)
			- Geographically-specialized fund
			- Positive for medium-term apprecation trend of Asian currencies
		- ### Asian Balanced Fund
			- Feeder fund
			- 50% Asia Pacific Equity, 25% Asian Bond Fund, 25% Asia Local  Bond Funds
			- TAA (+-10%)
			- Moderate risk profile
		- ### Asia Pacific Equity Fund
			- Invested in Asia-Pacific region (except Japan, involves Australia, New Zealand)
		- ### Global Emerging Markets Dynamic Fund
			- Invests in global emerging markets 
		- ### Cash Flow Fund
