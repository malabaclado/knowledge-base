The **union** of two sets $A$ and $B$ contains all the elements that is *either* in set $A$ or in set $B$.

The **intersection** of two sets $A$ and $B$ contains all the elements that are *both* in set $A$ and set $B$.

---

### Formal Definition
> **Definition.** Let $A$ and $B$ be sets.
> - The **union** of $A$ and $B$, denoted $A\cup B$ is defined as $$A\cup B = \{x | x\in A \text{ or }x\in B\} $$
> - The **intersection** of $A$ and $B$, denoted $A\cap B$, is defined as $$A\cap B = \{x |x\in A \text{ and } x\in B\}$$

---
### Properties
The properties of the union and intersection set operations include: *commutativity*, *associativity* and *distributivity*.


> **Proposition.** Let $A$, $B$ and $C$ be sets. Then
> 1. $A\cup B = B\cup A$
> 2. $A\cap B = B\cap A$
> 3. $A\cup (B \cup C) = (A\cup B) \cup C$
> 4. $A\cap (B \cap C) = (A\cap B) \cap C$
> 5. $A \cup (B\cap C) = (A\cup B)\cap (A\cup C)$
> 6. $A \cap (B\cup C) = (A\cap B)\cup (A\cap C)$
