# Getting ready for the corporate world (Carlos Evans)
- Speaker 1: [Juan Carlos Evans](https://hihello.me/p/d09dd7c7-0aa2-4d5b-acb8-ddbba4bdf610)
- Talent Specialist at P&G

> Preparing for your career starts from the your education.

Things you need to prepare:
1. Curriculum Vitae and Resume
2. LinkedIn profile


### How to enhance your CV and Resume
1. Include your **contact information** -
	- Include your contact number, active email, LinkedIn profile and permanent address.
2. On your CV - discuss yourself briefly
	- You have to think that you're the best among all the best applicants.
3. On work experience
	- Include only work experience that is relevant to the job you're applying for.
4. On education 
	- Include your course, school and award
5. Your skills
	- Include soft skills that might be relevant to the job you're applying. 
	- If you're on tech, include your stack and programming language skills.


### Tips to enhance your LinkedIn Profile
- Your headline should highlight your experience. It should be something specific than the title of your current profession.
- Your photo should be fresh and nice as it can affect judgments of traits such as competence and trustworthiness.
- Make a robust summary - 
- HIghlight your achievements 
	- If fresh grad, put your achievements and latin honors
	- If no work experience, add volunteering experiences
- Post regular updates
	- Think of things that would be relevant to your goal job
- Highlight your skills 
	- Make your skill section robust.
	- Add your certifications.

### Tips to ace the corporate interview
- Q: Is it advisable to ask for feedback (on the grounds of improving your weaknesses) after your interview?
- Simple answer framework: 
	- Context (What is the situation) 
	- Action (What did you do) 
	- Result (How did it help the company/project?)
		- Choose a positive result.
- Practicing behavioral interview questions:
	- Identify what you are being assessed of
	- Think of a story so you can answer with the Context-Action-Results Framework
	- Lead with your short answer then support your stand with a story.
- Sample Q: If you were given a difficult project, how would you ensure its success?
	- A: 
- Sample Q: Give us an example of a goal you failed to meet, and how you handled the situation.
	- Tip: This question assesses how you would try to avoid mistakes next time.
	- Sample A: There was one time where I am working on this project ... but then it didn't work out well so what I did was ...  so that next time ... and then the next opportunity came to me and then what I did was ... so that what happened is ... and it turned out great this time.
- Sample Q: What would you do if you disagree with someone at work?

### Other tips
- Q: Is it advisable to be friendly/chill with the interviewer or be strictly formal/professional?
	- A: You have to be professional. Be friendly only if the interviewer initiated it. 
- Ask what happens to the interview: Is it well? Is it fine? 
- Make sure to thank the interviewer at the end of the interview.

# Design your career as an evolving masterpiece
Speaker: [Kurly De Guzman](https://hihello.me/p/acd1f999-131e-4a5c-a9f7-6cff4266ce76)

- Design your career as an evolving masterpiece.
	- The path you choose today is not a fixed path. Be open to opportunities and do not be afraid to make changes to your career blueprint. 
	- There are jobs today that hadn't existed 20 years ago. The world of work is evolving, so are you! 
- We need others to thrive.
	- Trust is essential for psychological safety
		- Example: When you made a mistake in work. When you're not afraid to make mistakes (such as when making suggestions), that means you are in a psychologically safe space.
	- ![](https://i.imgur.com/Qh0yxut.png)
- 4C's to be a team player
	- Collaboration
		- Get to know your team genuinely. 
		- Knowing them personally - beyond the workplace - can mutually improve work experience
		- Seek help when needed
		- Network - people are more than their jobs , 
		- Get their inputs 
		- Communicate openly - you get feedback and you give feedback.
	- Commitment
		- You don't want to be someone who doesn't follow-up on their word. 
		- Only make commitments you can keep, keep the commitments you made and communicate when you can no longer make it.
	- Curiosity
		- You allow others to speak, seek their ideas.
		- Sometimes we have biases and let our assumptions get ahead of us. Do not finish what they are saying in your head or through your words.
		- Be ready to listen - not only to what they're sayong - but also to what they are not saying: non-verbal cues, actions and expressions.
		- Show respect for others.
	- Celebration
		- Express appreciation and gratitude
		- Tell someone when he/she did a good job.
		- *In my experience: Simple appreciation can make or break someone's dream. I experience this when a classmate told me I have a great potential to be a teacher even though I believe myself that I have no passion on teaching. This conversation makes me think about it and consider a teaching career in the future.*

# Other Tips (Q&A)
- Q: How can one be truly self aware? Like there are times when you feel like you already know yourself but you come off as someone different in other people's eyes
	- You ask for feedback. If a lot of people say a specific thing about you, then it might probably be true.
	- Take personality assessments. This can either be a validation of something that you already know or give you insight moving forward.
	- Take personal assessments. Record your thoughts and evaluate yourself through journalling, meditation.
- Q: How do we deal with conflicting personalities with our co-workers? Should we just give in if they do not want to compromise/they're too aggresive?
	- When there are conflicts, its always helpful to practice curiosity.
		- Conflicts usually arise from misunderstanding. So it is very important to get a complete understanding of the situation.
		- Find out where the problem is coming from. Do not polarize. 
- Define success in your own way.
