# Lecture 25-29
Analytic Projective Geometry 


The coordinate of a point in the real projective plane is the direction vector of the line (that passes through the origin in R3) associated with the point.

![[Pasted image 20220427104827.png]]


There is 1-1 correspondence bet the lines (passing through the origin) in R3 and points in pi(R)

We can associate a direction vector <x,y,z> to line l in R3. We associate this vector to the point P in pi(R). 

The coordinates of a point in the projective plane is not unique. Any multiple of the direction vector can identify the same point.

For any point P(x,y,z), if z=0, then P is an ideal point.

==Lec 26==

Lines in the real projective plane pi(R) is formed by the intersection of a plane in R3 to pi(R). 

![[Pasted image 20220427110241.png]]

There is a correspondence between the planes in R3 passing throught the origin to the lines in pi(R). 

We associate normal vector of the plane to lines in pi(R). The normal vector has coordinates <a,b,c>. The line associated with the normal vector <a,b,c> is the line l [a,b,c]. (We use brackets to distinguish coordinates of lines against points)

Coordinates of lines in pi(R) are not unique -> the multiple of the normal vectors associated with the plane is still a normal vector of that plane. 

![[Pasted image 20220427110819.png]]

Incidence of Point and Lines in the Projective Plane. 
- A point is on a line if the vectors associated with their coordinates are orthogonal. This will happen if the dot product of the coordinates is zero. That is, $$\langle a,b,c\rangle \cdot \langle x,y,z \rangle = ax+by+cz =0$$

==Lec 28== 

The ideal point on the line ax+by+cz = 0
- Equation of the ideal point: ax+by = 0 
- Ideal point coordinate: (x,y,0) = (x,-a/b, 0) = (1,-a/b,0) = (b,-a,0)

![[Pasted image 20220427123522.png]]

The point with coordinate (b,-a,0) is the ideal point of all lines in the family of lines represented by the equation $ax+by+c=0$
