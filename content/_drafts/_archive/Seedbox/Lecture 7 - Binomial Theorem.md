# Lecture 7 - Binomial Theorem
## Binomial Theorem 

For all $n\in \mathbb{N}$ and $x,y \in \mathbb{R}$, 
$$(x+y)^{n} = \binom{n}{0}x^{n} + \binom{n}{1}x^{n-1} +\binom{n}{2}x^{n-2}y^{2} +...+ \binom{n}{n}y^{n}$$
or concisely, 
$$(x+n)^{n} = \sum_{r=0}^{n} \binom{n}{r}x^{n-r}y^{r} $$



> [!attention] Remark
> 1. We define $0^{0}=1$, by convention, so that $(x+y)^{0}= 1$ even when $(x+y)=0$. This is only for combinatorics.
> 2. For $n,r \in \mathbb{Z}$ with $n\geq 0$, $\binom{n}{r}$ is called the *binomial coefficient*.
