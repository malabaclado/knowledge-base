Effective data science and analytics project management ensure that the organization, big or small, will be able to reap the benefits of a well-planned and properly managed project, resulting in satisfied stakeholders and achievement of business objectives. Participants of the course gain knowledge on project management processes, the efforts required for each process group, and best practices to successfully implement data science and analytics projects. Through exercises and use case discussions, participants move toward understanding the concepts, to practical applications.

# Intro to Project Management
- ## Definitions
	- What is a project?
		- A project is a temporary endeavor undertaken to create a unique product, service or result.
		- ==Key: A project has an end.==
	- Who are project managers?
		- They make project goals
		- They can see the big picture and also the small details
		- They cultivate skills needed to develop trust and communication among all the project's stakeholders.
	- Stakeholders
		- People whose interest may be positively or negatively affected as a result of project execution or successful project implementation.
	- Project Sponsor
		- A person who owns the project
		- Provides resources and support for the project
	- What is a data science/analytics project
		- A project that elivers value to stakeholders using data, big or small.
- Two popular frameworks for project management
	- 1. Traditional (AKA Waterfall)
		- Linear, sequential project life cycle
		- Advantages
			- Best for simple to less complex projects
			- Well documented
			- ==Helpful in managing dependencies==
		- Limitations
			- Not ideal for large complex projects
			- Difficult to make changes (everything is planned at the start of planning process)
			- Testing only happens at the end of developement and bugs will be expensive to fix. (Which would cause delays and more cost)
			- Customer only see the finished product at the end of the project.
		- Suitable for the following indusry: Construction
	- 2. Agile2023-07-22 11:18 AM
		- Continuous and iterative 
		- Represented by a wheel - you can always go back and revise the product.
		- Advantages
			- Focused on customer needs
			- Teams are self-organized
			- Process is incremental based
			- Customers have high visibility on the progress of the product.
		- Limitations
			- Requires quick decision making from a strong product owner
			- Projects can easily go off-track without strong leadership and guidance
			- Works best if you have dedicated project teams.
- ## Types of Agile Framework
	- Scrum
		- Scrum is a framework to address complex adaptive problems while productivelt and creativelt delivering products of the highest possible value.
		- Scrum is NOT a process nor a technique for building products; rather it is a framework within which you can employ various processes and techniques.
	- Sprint 
		- is a time box of 1 month or less during which, a done or usable product increment is created. 
		- The quality goals do not decrease as well during the sprint.
		- Sprint Planning
			- Sprints are kicked off by sprint planning
			- During this phase, the team would answer the ff questions:
				- 1. What will be delivered in the increment resulting from the upcoming sprint?
				- 2. How will the work needed to deliver the increment be achieved?
			- Plan collaborative effort in order to commit and deliver what is requested.
		- Daily Scrum
			- A 15-min timebox event held every day.
			- Here, the development team plans work in the next 24 hours.
			- During this phase, the team would answer the ff questions:
				- 1. What have I done yesterday?
				- 2. What do I do today?
				- 3. What are the problems encountered by me or by the team?
		- Sprint Review
			- Purpose: inspect the outcome of the sprint and determine future adaptations
			- Stakeholders are present during sprint review
			- The scrum team presents their work.
		- Sprint Retro
			- Opportunity for the scrum team to inspect itself and create a plan and improvements to be enacted during the next sprint.
	- Kanban
		- Framework to implement agile in a continuous and more fluid setting.
		- This agile methodology is not necessarily iterative but is incremental.
		- Have *Work in progress* limits which is based on the capacity that keeps the project team focused on only a portion  of the work at any given time.
	- CRISP-DM
		- Stands for Cross-Industry Standard Process for Data Mining
		- Originally designed for data mining.
		- A 6-phase lifecycle
		- Provides a complete blueprint for conducting a data science/mining project.

- ## When and where to use each framework
	- Waterfall: When requirements are **clear and fixed**, the product is **stable**, and the technology stack is well understood.
	- Agile -Scrum: When doing large projects.It divides larger projects into a series of mini-projects, each with a consistent and fixed length.
	- CRISM-DM: This is a good starting point when forming a new data science team.

# Initiation: How and Where to Start a Project

- ## Why are projects initiated?
	- Due to **market demand**
	- Due to **customer feedback**
	- Due to a **strategic opportunity**
	- Because of **legal or compliance regulations**
	- To address **social needs**
	- To drive **advancement in technology**
	- For **environmental considerations**
- ## Project Initiation
	- ==*Always start with the "why"*==
	- ## Why is initiation important?
		- It formally **authorizes a project to begin**
		- Ensures that the initiative will **add value**
		- **Offers a structured approach** to demonstrate the project's business case **and prove that the work to be done is feasible**.
		- Sets the target for the project, **provides high level goals and objectives**.
	- ## What is a business case?
		- A business case **is a justification for a project or an initiative**.
		- Establishes a project's tangible and intangible objectives.
		- Will help the organization decide whether or not to move forward with the budget.
	- ## Business case outline
		- Project Name
		- Executive summary
		- Current process
		- Reason to change
		- Options considered
		- Benefits
		- Timescales
		- Cost
		- Expected return on investment
		- Risks
	- ## What is a project charter?
		- A project charter **is a formal document that describes the project in its entirety.**
		- Includes what objectives are, how it will be carried out, and who the stakeholders are.
		- It effectively authorizes the project and appoints the project manager.
	- ## Business case vs Project charter
		- Business case is created first before the project charter
		- Business case provides what impacts the project to the business will be and is used to determine whether the project is worth pursuing.
		- Project charter draws from the business case for the financial parameteres needed to operate the project. It tells what needs to be done at a high level, and what the constraints are in terms of budget, timeline and scope.
	- ## Project inititation tips
		- 1. Identify the project manager.
		- 2. Collect historical information if available.
		- 3. Divide large projects into phases, releases or identify the MVP.
		- 4. Determine high level requirements, constraints, assumptions and risks
		- 5. Determine the success or acceptance criteria
		- 6. Perform high level estimation on schedule and budget.
		- 7. Identify stakeholders and their level of influence
		- 8. Finalize the charter and have it approved
		- 9. Define the exit criteria for the project.
	- ## Project Charter Outline
		- 1. Title page (+ Date + Name of project manager)
		- 2. Overview : What the project is about
		- 3. Goals
		- 4. Assumptions
		- 5. Deliverables
		- 6. Key stakeholders
		- 7. Success metrics
	- Key takeaways:
		- 1. A business case may be required to justify and prioritize your data science project
		- 2. Project Charters are created and approved in order to authorize the project.
		- Always understand *why* of the project.

# Planning: What to look out at start of the project
- ==*If you fail to plan, you plan to fail*==
- ## What is project planning?
	- **Project planning** is the most important step in project management.
	- It entails going through a project using a **consistent and iterative** process to **get the project organized in sufficient detail** before actually doing the work needed to produce the product.
	- This is where the project manager conduct a **detailed analysis of the objectives** of the project and whether the expected benefits can be achieved.
- ## Why is project planning important?
	- ==Effective project planning efforts save on resources, time and money.==
	- It encourages stakeholder buy-in and commitment to the project.
	- It **results in a documented project management plan** which will serve as a guide throughout the execusion of the project.
- ## How much time should you spend on planning?
	- It depends on multiple factors. 
	- But in general, if the project is **high-priority** with a **tight schedule** with no room for variance, then the project will require more planning than a **low priority** project with **flexible schedule**.

	- 2. Define and prioritize a product backlog

## Steps in Project Planning
Planning your project involves the process of defining your objectives and scope, goals and budget for resources.

### Step 1. Determine the approach or life cycle
A project life cycle is a logical breakdown of what you need to do to produce the deliverable of the project.

### Step 2. Define and prioritize product backlog
A product backlog is a laundry list of all the things to be done in the project. It acts as the visual representation of the development process and allows team members of the current status of the project.

#### MOSCOW analysis
Is a framework for prioritizing product backlog designed to prioritize important tasks that provide the highest business value.
- M: Must-have - these are non-negotiable, mandatory 
- S: Should have - non-vital but important
- C: Could-have - nice-to-have
- W: Would not have - not a priority

MVP is a version of the product that's just enough to satisfy the customer's requirements.

### Step 4. Create the scope statement
A Project Scope Statement should answer the following questions:
- WHAT will you do?
- WHY will you do it?
- WHAT will you not do? *(Out of scope tasks)*

### Step 5. Define the project team
After you defined the project scope, its now time to define the project team. This team will help the project manager identify the complexities and the dependencies of eachof the requirements set up at the beginning of project planning - so that the project manager will have a high-level overview of the tasks needed to be done in the project.

### Step 6. Break down the large requiremetns into workable tasks
In scrum, this step is known as "backlog refinement".

![[Pasted image 20230810090651.png|300]]

### Step 7. Estimate the complexity of each tasks
**User Story Estimation** is a unit of measure by project teams to measure the complexity of the user story. 

The Fibonacci Sequence (1,2,3,5,8,13) is the most commonly used set of estimates. (1 is the easiest, 13 is the most difficult)

![[Pasted image 20230810090801.png|300]]

### Step 8. Determing the dependencies and capacity
Identify the prerequisites for each item (dependency) and list them out. 
![[Pasted image 20230810091347.png|300]]

### Step 9. Develop high level cost and schedule estimates
The project manager should now arrange the user stories according to priority and in order of dependencies.

- Schedule estimates - including the necessary wait time from dependencies per requirement.
- Cost estimates - including the cost for tools, infrastrucure, and manpower costs.

### Step 10. Determine quality metrics, standards and processes.
- Get to know the different quality standards (types of testing) for every organization.
- Identify which of the standards and processes apply to your project.
- For data science and analytics projects, these may include model validation and A/B testing.

### Step 11. Assign roles and responsibilities
- Stakeholders
	- These are the parties involved or has interest in the project.
	- Map out what each stakeholder will do to help with the project.
	- Use RACI Matrix for this step.
		- R - assigned to the person responsible for the task completion
		- A - assigned to the person accountable for making decisions and making actions in the task
		- C - assigned to the person to be consulted regarding the decision making process and specific task
		- I - assigned to the person to be informed of the decisions/actions made during the project

![[Pasted image 20230810091949.png|300]]

### Step 12. Plan communications and stakeholder management
In large projects, it is important that your communication with stakeholders is properly planned.

- Description of communication: *What type of communication is it?*
- Frequency: *How often will you be communicating?*
- Channel: *Which platform would you be communication?*
- Audience: *Who is receiving the communication?*
- Owner: *Who is in charge of sending out the communication?*

![[Pasted image 20230810092520.png|300]]

### Step 13. Complete risk assessment
Risk VS Issue
- Risk - hasn't happened yet but has some probability of happening; potential problems
- Issue - has already happened

This process involves identifying possible risks, the probability of it occuring and their impact to the project.

Once you have established the risks and their gravity, **develop a risk response plan**, which outlines responses and contingency plans should risk occur.

Doing so minimizes the impact of major threats to the project.

![[Pasted image 20230810092903.png]]

### Step 14. Review and iterate
No plan is final. Always go back and check if there are things that can be further improved.

- **In agile-scrum model**, project execution happens during the actual sprint,
- **In CRISP-DM model**, execution happens within Data Preparation and Modelling phase.

## Tips in Project Execution
1. Execute according to plan, achieving results that meet requirements.
2. Assess and evaluate change requests. Implement only the approved changes.
3. Continuous improvement. Share lessons learned. Do sprint retrospective.
4. Perform quality audits. Ensures the team is performing according to requirements
5. Manage the team. Make sure the teams are equipped with necessary tools to complete their work.
6. Use issue log - which is a document where all issues negatively affecting the project are recorded and tracked.
7. Report on project performance
	1. Keep track of your project's health
	2. Summarize project progress
	3. Maximize project management tool
	4. Identify risks and blockers
	5. Add links if document attachments are needed.
8. Facilitate stakeholder engagement and manage expectations.
9. Execute a contingency plan, as necessary.




# Monitoring, Controlling and Closing

## Project Monitoring and Controlling
Executing VS Monitoring VS Controlling
- Executing: "Executing according to plan"
- Monitoring: "Verifying that the execution is going according to plan"
- Controlling: "Ensuring that any variation from the plan is within acceptable limits"

**Monitoring** is the collection, recording and reporting of project information during the project execution phase. **Controlling** requires evaluating hard data on how the project is conforming to the plan and takig action to address variances that are outside the acceptable limits.

> *Expect the best, plan for the worst and prepare to be surprised.*

When does Monitoring and Controlling happen?
- In waterfall model, monitoring and controlling happens parallel during project execution.
- In agile scrum,monitoring and controlling happens during sprints, especially highlighted during Daily Scrums.
- In CRISM-DM, monitoring and controlling happen during Data Understanding, Data Preparation, Modelling and Evaluation.

Tips in Monitoring and Controlling Phase
1. Measure project performance (scope, schedule, budget, quality standards) according to planned measures.
2. Analyze and evaluate work performance data. Is the project still within scope, schedule, budget, quality standards?
3. Control to the baselines or adjust as necessary. Implicate changes to the project plan and make sure to have these changes approved.
4. Determine whether variances as applicable. Are these variances acceptable? What are the root causes of the problem and what can you do about it? Also record lessons learned along the way.
5. Hold periodic quality inspections.
6. Make decisions to accept or reject completed deliverables.
7. Look for arising risks, while analyzing existing risks.

## Controlling changes
*What to do when changes are inevitable?*
- Evaluate the impact of the change to the project.
- Identify possible options to consider.
- Get the change approved by the higher-ups.
- Get customer buy-in.

## Project Closing
Project closing is the assurance that all the work has been completed and involves handing over the deliverables to the customer. 

It is a formal recognition of the completion of a project - and where everyone agrees that it is completed.

A project closes when any of the three happens:
1. Project phase is complete.
2. Project is fully complete.
3. Project is terminated (might be due to budget cut/objective became obsolete)
