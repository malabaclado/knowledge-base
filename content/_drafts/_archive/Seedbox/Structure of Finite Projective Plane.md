# Structure of Finite Projective Plane
![[Pasted image 20220303232203.png|400]]

Basically, if a line has $n$ points, then all lines have $n$ points and that there are $n^2-n+1$ points in the plane.

Remarks:
- The smalles projective plane contains at least three points and at least three lines. 
- This theorem has its own dual.

---
## Example
- In 7-point Projective Plane, there are exactly 3 points on each line. Thus, there is $3^{2}-3+1 = 7$ points 
- In 13-point projective plane, there is $4^{4}- 4 +1 = 13$ lines. 

---
## Dual of the Theorem
Let $n\geq 3, n \in \mathbb{N}$. In a projective plane, the following are equivalent:
1. There is point that lies in exactly $n$ lines.
2. All points have $n$ lines passing through it.
3. The number of lines in the plane is $n^{2}-n +1$.
