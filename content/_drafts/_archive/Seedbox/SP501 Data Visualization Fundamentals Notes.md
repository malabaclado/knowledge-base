> [!NOTE]+ Course Overview
> The role of data visualization is to take information and present it visually to make it understandable to your audience. For this course, we will focus on visual communication. Building up from your previous course on **Essential Excel Skills for Data Preparation and Analysis**, we now move into the application of visualizations and data storytelling. We will begin with an overview of Data Visualization, exploring resource material, and what we need for visual communication. In the second week, we will discuss the Types of Data Stories. This will be followed by the discussion on general applications of Charts and Graphs by Type of Data Story. To cap off the course, we will discuss Chart Design Principles and Data Visualization Ethics to guide us in our work.

- ## Types of Data Visualization
	- ### Composition
		- Composition shows how data is a part of a whole
		- Pie Chart, 100% Stacked Bar Chart, Stacked Area Chart 100%, Waterfall Chart
	- ### Comparison
		- Comparison shows the dependence between two or more data
		- Line Chart, Column Chart, Bar Chart, Two-axis Chart
	- ### Distribution
		- Line Chart
		- Column Chart
		- Scatter Plot
		- Two-Axis Chart
	- ### Relationship
		- Scatter Plot
		- Bubble Chart
		- Two-Axis Chart

# Week 4. Chart Design Principles and Data Visualization Ethics
- ## Chart Design Principles
	- ### Be Concise
		- Declutter your charts. 
		- Simplicity is key (KISS principle)
		- Maximize chart title
	- ### Prepare the content
		- Format numbers properly.
		- Sort data logically.
		- Use data tables *when necessary*.
	- ### Establish Context
		- Use more than one chart *when necessary*
		- Use the proper type of chart for the data
		- Use a reasonable aspect ratio

- ## Data Visualization Ethics
	- Know your audience
		- *"Instead of thinking about the audience as a nified clump when preparing your presentationm imagine them as a line of individuals waiting to have a face-to-face with you."*
		- Guide questions:
			- *What do they need to know?*
			- *Who are your audience?*
			- *Who you are. (Establish your credibility and trustworthiness of the source of information)*
	- Know the goal of the meeting
		- When presenting data, it's essential to distinguish between exploratory and explanatory analysis. Exploratory analysis involves delving into data to uncover and highlight interesting or noteworthy observations for the audience. In contrast, explanatory analysis aims to communicate a specific, focused point derived from your analysis, rather than presenting a broad range of highlights. Therefore, understanding your objective is crucial; if your goal is to tell a particular story and prompt a specific action from your audience, you are engaging in explanatory analysis.
	- Show accurate data
		- Avoid faulty visualizations
			- Bar charts start from zero.
			- Line charts are on same interval.
	- Urge to decide
		- Provide (the audience) informaton to make a decision.
		- The audience should learn something from the presenter.
