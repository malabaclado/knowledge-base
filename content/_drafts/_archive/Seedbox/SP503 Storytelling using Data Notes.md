## Data storytelling is a form of communication
Though often mistaken as data visualization, **data storytelling** is beyond tools, it is a form of communication that a data storyteller should master in order to succeed in connecting to its audiences and effectively conquer their interests and focus during a presentation.

## Three core elements of data storytelling
![[Pasted image 20230906112155.png|400]]
- **Data** is the information with which you've drawn insights from.
- **Visuals** is the representation of your data.
- **Narrative** is the story behnd your data.
- Adding ***visuals*** to your ***data*** should **enlighten** your audience to information even without you speaking.
- Adding ***narrative*** to your ***data*** should help **explain** your message and its context to your audience
- Use both ***narrative*** and ***visuals*** to keep your audience entertained and **engaged**.

## Principles of Effective Content

- Three principles for creating appealing content: Purpose, People, and Preparation.
- Formulate a precise objective for your presentation.
- Guide questions to ask: 
	- What do I want to do? 
	- What do I want my audience to know? 
	- What do I want my audience to do? 
	- How do I want my audience to feel?

### Purpose

The "Purpose" principle is a fundamental concept in creating effective content. It revolves around having a clear and well-defined objective for your content, ensuring that every piece of material you produce serves a specific purpose. This principle is essential for various forms of content, whether it's a blog post, a marketing campaign, a presentation, or any other type of communication.

At its core, the "Purpose" principle requires you to articulate the reason why you are creating the content. It prompts you to ask critical questions about what you intend to achieve with it. For instance, are you trying to inform, educate, entertain, persuade, or inspire your audience? Defining your purpose sets the direction for the entire content creation process.

- Create an outline with all the elements you want to include.
- Consider your audience to achieve your objective and associate your personality with your message positively.
- Focus on what you want the audience to take away.
### People
The "People" aspect of effective content focuses on understanding and connecting with your target audience. This principle recognizes that successful content is not just about what you want to say; it's also about who you're speaking to and how well you resonate with them.

1. **Audience-Centric Approach**: The "People" aspect emphasizes putting your audience at the center of your content strategy. It starts with gaining a deep understanding of your target audience. This includes demographic information (age, gender, location), psychographics (interests, values, beliefs), pain points, and preferences. The more you know about your audience, the better you can tailor your content to their needs and interests.
2. **Relevance and Value**: Effective content must be relevant and valuable to your audience. It should address their questions, concerns, and challenges. By focusing on what matters most to your audience, you can create content that resonates and provides real benefits. This helps in building trust and credibility with your audience.
3. **Audience Segmentation**: In many cases, your target audience is not homogenous. They may have different needs and preferences within the same group. Audience segmentation involves categorizing your audience into smaller, more specific subgroups based on shared characteristics. This allows you to create content that speaks directly to each segment, increasing its relevance and impact.
4. **Empathy and Emotional Connection**: Effective content often taps into the emotions of your audience. It's not just about delivering information but also about how you make your audience feel. Content that evokes emotions, such as empathy, curiosity, excitement, or inspiration, tends to be more memorable and shareable.
5. **Language and Tone**: The "People" aspect also considers the language and tone you use in your content. Your communication style should align with your audience's preferences. For instance, a formal tone might be suitable for academic content, while a more conversational tone might work better for a younger, casual audience.
6. **Feedback and Engagement**: Listening to your audience is crucial. Encourage feedback, comments, and engagement with your content. Pay attention to what your audience is saying, and use their input to refine your content strategy. This iterative approach helps you continually improve and adapt your content to better serve your audience.
7. **Personalization**: Tailoring content to individual preferences and behaviors is becoming increasingly important. Personalization involves using data and technology to deliver content that is highly relevant to each user. This can lead to higher engagement and conversion rates.


### Preparation
The "preparation" aspect of effective content refers to the planning, organization, and careful consideration that goes into creating high-quality and impactful content. This principle recognizes that content creation is not a haphazard process but requires thorough preparation to ensure that the final product meets its objectives and resonates with the target audience.

- Preparation begins as soon as you agree to the presentation.
- Pay attention to details such as when, where, and to how many people you are giving the presentation.
- Consider whether it's remote or in-person and what attire is appropriate.
- Write a script for your presentation.
- Add variety to keep the audience interested.
- Rehearse to build confidence.
- Seek feedback to improve performance.
- Avoid dwelling on what didn't work and play to your strengths.
- Sticking to your objectives is crucial in preparation.

## Structure of an effective presentation

**The Law of Three**
The "Law of Three" is a rhetorical principle and a communication technique often used in public speaking, storytelling, and writing. It suggests that information or ideas presented in groups of three are more engaging, satisfying, and memorable to audiences compared to other numerical groupings.

- **Opening, body and conclusion**. A presentation should follow a rigid structure consisting of three main parts: the opening, the body, and the closing. This structure aligns with the "Law of Three," as it divides the presentation into three distinct sections. Each section has its own purpose and contributes to the overall effectiveness of the presentation.
- **Engage the audience**. The opening of the presentation is crucial and that a speaker has a short window of time (four to seven seconds) to capture the audience's attention. This aligns with the "Law of Three" in the sense that the opening should be concise, engaging, and clear. The speaker must achieve multiple objectives in this brief period, including making a good first impression, giving a preview, and inviting the audience to be comfortable.

#### **Tips when opening a presentation.**
1. Craft an engaging opening line to **capture the audience's attention right from the start**.
2. **Begin with a brief introduction of yourself **to establish credibility and confidence.
3. **Invite the audience to be comfortable** and at ease during your presentation.
4. **Provide a brief overview** or preview of what the audience can expect from your presentation.
5. Use techniques to **grab the audience's attention** and make them interested in your topic.
6. If necessary, **provide clear instructions on how the audience should engage or participate** during the presentation.
7. Ensure that your opening smoothly transitions into the main body of your presentation.
8. Unless you're confident in your humor, it's generally advisable to **avoid starting with a joke.**
9. **Refrain from apologizing at the beginning** unless it's part of a deliberate humorous device.
10. **Begin your presentation promptly** as a sign of respect to those who arrived on time.
11. **Outline the structure of your presentation early on**, including the topic, introduction, and key points.
12. **Clarify any essential terminology** to ensure the audience is not lost during the presentation.
13. **Present a clear and direct thesis statement** that encapsulates your main topic and purpose.

These tips can help you create a strong and engaging opening for your presentation, setting the tone for a successful communication with your audience.

#### **Tips when presenting the body of presentation**
1. Ensure that the body of your presentation is **well-structured and follows a logical order**. Use appropriate headings and subheadings to organize your content.
2. The body of your presentation **should consist of your main points and subpoints**. These provide the essential information, details, and evidence to support your main idea or topic.
3. The structure of the body doesn't vary significantly between oral and written presentations. **Maintain a consistent approach in organizing your content.**
4. **Divide the body into sections, with each section corresponding to the points on your outline.** This helps maintain clarity and coherence in your presentation.
5. **Use each heading within the body to develop key points**. Ask yourself what the main point is and what facts or evidence support it.
6. Enhance your presentation by incorporating references to **authoritative sources or experts**. Additionally, personalize your content by sharing **personal experiences** or providing **vivid descriptions**.
7. **Utilize signposts to represent ideas**, products, or services. Signposting also helps your audience understand the structure of your presentation and what to expect next.
8. Consider using examples, graphs, tables, charts, and other supporting references to enhance the analysis and understanding of your content.
9. **Develop your arguments in a logical structure** within the body of your presentation. Clearly state claims or assumptions that align with the purpose of your presentation.
10. **Aim for smooth transitions between paragraphs** to maintain the flow of your presentation. Each paragraph should focus on making one clear point. Start a new paragraph when addressing a different aspect of the same point.

#### **Tips when closing a presentation**

1. **Start and End Strong:** Ensure that your presentation has a strong beginning and ending.
2. **Convince Your Audience:** Remember that it's your responsibility to persuade and convince your audience with your conclusion.
3. **Summarize Main Points:** Use the conclusion to summarize the main points of your presentation.
4. **Provide Closure:** Give your audience a sense of closure, indicating that your presentation is coming to an end.
	- To provide closure in your presentation's conclusion, you should succinctly **recap the main points you've covered throughout your presentation, remind your audience of the central message or argument you've conveyed**, and explain why the information is significant or relevant. 
	- This step essentially brings your audience back to the core of your presentation, reinforces key takeaways, and helps them understand the broader importance of what they've learned, ensuring that your message sticks and your presentation feels complete.
5. **Leave an Impression:** Make sure your closing leaves a lasting impression on your audience.
6. **Engage with Questions:** Consider asking your audience for questions before officially closing to maintain engagement.
7. **Assert Your Argument:** Reiterate your main argument or point strongly to leave a lasting impact.
8. **Use Familiar Cues:** Consider using cues from your introduction, such as a quote, question, image, anecdote, or emotional sharing, to tie your presentation together.
	- For instance, you can reiterate a quote, question, image, anecdote, or emotional story that you initially used to capture your audience's attention at the beginning of your presentation. By bringing these cues full circle, you connect the start and end of your presentation, reinforcing key ideas and leaving a lasting impression. 
	- This technique not only reinforces the central message but also helps the audience recall and relate to the presentation's content more effectively, enhancing the overall impact of your conclusion.
9. **Invite Further Discussion:** Encourage ongoing discussion or engagement, possibly by sharing your contact information with the audience.
