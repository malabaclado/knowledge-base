**In several organizations, Analytics is treated as an IT function. However, for an organization to realize the full potential of Analytics, it should be treated as a business function.** 

All Analytics initiatives should be driven by the business and should be aligned to the organization’s vision, mission, and overall objectives. If not, the leadership team will see little value in these Analytics projects.


> [!NOTE] Objective
> - To be able to outline an Analytics strategy road map for your organization by following a use case showing how to effectively execute Analytics initiatives through the alignment of Analytics outputs to the organization’s overall objectives.

- What is a strategy?
	- It is a description of the overall way in which an organization currently is, and is to run. 
	- It covers a year at a time.
	- It **aligns** the organization's business area, resources, and activities to the market in which the organization operates.
	- Keyword: **alignment** (strategy -> objectives)
	- It attempts to handle issues in the short run while, at the same time, trying to create comptetive advantages in the long run.

How do you start?
- In most cases, you ned to do a *proof of concept* first to show your leaders that there is value in analytics.
- To do a proof of concept, select a project from a specific department. 

Key takeaways
- Analytics initiatives of a unit or a departmenr must support and promote the department's overall startegic targets, which equally must support and promote the overall sttartegic targets of the organization as a whole. 
- All underlying contributions and activities must submit to the chosen analytics strategy.
- The analytics strategy is decided at the top leadership level based on the organization's vision, mission and overall objectives.
