# Properties of the determinant

> [!NOTE] Theorem
> If a  matrix has a row or column of zeroes, then $\text{det } A=0$ 

> [!NOTE] Theorem
> If $A$ is a square matrix, then the determinant of $A$ is equal to its transpose. $$\text{det }A = \text{det }A^{T}$$

> [!NOTE] Theorem
> If a square matrix $A = [a_{ij}] \in M_{n}(\mathbb{F})$ is upper or lower triangular, then the determinant of A: $$\text{det }A = \Pi^{n}_{i=1}a_{ii}$$

> [!NOTE] Theorem
> If a matrix $B$ is obtained as a product of a square matrix $A \in M_{n}(\mathbb{F})$ and a scalar $c \in \mathbb{F}$, then the determinant of $B$ is $$\text{det }B = c \cdot \text{det }A$$

> [!NOTE] Theorem
> If a matrix $B$ is obtained by interchanging two rows (or columns) of matrix $A$, then $$\text{det }B = - \text{det }A$$
As a corollary to the above theorem: If two rows (or columns) of $A \in M_{n}(\mathbb{F})$ are equal, then $\text{det }A =0$.
