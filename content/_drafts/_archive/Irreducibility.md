> ==Definition== (Irreducibility over an Integral Domain)
> Let $D$ be an intrgral domain. A nonconstant polynomial $f(x)\in D[x]$ is said to be **irreducible over D** if in any factorization $g(x)h(x)$ of $f(x)$, where $g(x),h(x) \in D[x]$ either $g(x)$ or $h(x)$ is a unit in D[x]. Otherwise, f(x) is said to be reducible over D[x].

> ==Definition== (Irreducibility over a Field F)
> Let F be a field. A nonconstant polynomial $f(x)\in F[x]$ is **reducible over F** if f(x) can be written as a product $g(x)h(x)$ where $$0<\text{deg } g, \text{deg } h <\text{deg } f$$ Otherwise, f(x) is irreducible over F.

Remarks
- Every linear polynomial over a field F is irreducible over F
- Reducible polynomials over an integral domain are necessarily factorable. However, factorability does not imply reducibility.

# Irreducibility Tests
## Reducibility Test for Polynomials of Degree 2 or 3

> ==Theorem== (Reducibility Test for Polynomials of Degree 2 or 3)
> If $f(x) \in F[x]$ is of degree 2 or 3, then $f(x)$ is reducible over $F$ if and only if $f(x)$ has a zero in $F$.

## Rational Root Theorem

> ==Theorem== (Rational Root Theorem)
> Let $f(x)=a_nx^n + ,..., a_1x + a_0 \in \mathbb{Z}[x]$. If p/q is a rational zero of $f(x)$, then $p|a_0$ and $q|a_n$

## Primitive Polynomial and Gauss's  Lemma

>==Definition== (Content of a Polynomial, Primitive Polynomial)
> Let $f(x)=a_nx^n + ,..., a_1x + a_0 \in \mathbb{Z}[x]$. The ==content== of f(x) is the greatest common divisor of the coefficients of f(x). We say that f(x) is ==primitive== if its content is 1.


> ==Theorem== (Gauss's Lemma)
> The product of two primitive polynomials is primitive.

Every monic polynomial is primitive.

## Reducibility over Q implies Reducibility over Z
> ==Theorem== (Reducibility over Q -> Reducibility over Z)
> Let $f(x) \in F[x]$. If $f(x)$ is reducible over $Q$, then $f(x)$ is reducible over $Z$.

## Tests for Irreducibility over Q

### Mod p Irreducibility Test
> ==Theorem== (Mod p Irreducibility Test)
> - Let p be a prime, $f(x)$ be a nonconstant polynomial in $F[x]$
> - Let $\bar{f}(x)$ be the polynomial with reduced coeficients of f mod p.
> 
> If $\bar{f}(x)$ is irreducible over $\mathbb{Z}_p$, and $\text{deg }f = \text{deg } \bar{f}$, then f is irreducible over $\mathbb{Q}$.


---
### Eisenstein's Criterion for Irreducibility over Q

> ==Theorem== (Eisenstein's Criterion for Irreducibility over Q)
> Let $f(x)=a_nx^n +,...,+ a_1 x +a_0$
> If there exists $p$ such that:
> 1. $p$ does not divide $a_n$
> 2. $p$ divides $a_{n-1}, a_{n-2},...,a_1,a_0$
> 3. $p^2$ does not divide $a_0$
> 
> Then **f(x) is irreducible over Q**.

> ==Theorem==
> Let $p(x) \in F[x]$ be irreducible.
> 1. If $p(x)|g(x)h(x)$, then $p(x)|g(x)$ or $p(x)|h(x)$.
> 2. If $p(x)|g_1(x), g_2(x),..., g_k(x)$, then $p(x)|g_i(x)$ for $i=1,2,...,k$

> ==Theorem== (Unique Factorizarion of Polynomials)
> Every nonconstant polynomial $f(x)\in F[x]$ is either irreducible or is a product of irreducible polynomials in $F[x]$. These products (also called the factorization of $f(x)$ over $F[x]$) is unique  EXCEPT on units and order of factors.
